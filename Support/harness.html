What to look at

  1. Hover each block in turn — paragraph, heading, table, list, blockquote, code. The </> and the + appear together, and line up down the margin. A table is the one that has been broken before, and a list item is the one that drifts.
  2. Open a block, change it, press ⌘↵. The document below re-renders from the edited text, the way it does when Swift writes the file.
  3. Then hover another block. The control has to still be there. It once vanished for the rest of the session after the first successful edit.
  4. Open a block and press Esc; open one and press Done; open one and click away. All three leave the document readable.
  5. Read past an underlined quote without clicking. Its note appears over the words after a moment, with its tail on them, and goes when you move on — but moving onto the card keeps it, a card you clicked stays put, and one with its markdown open never closes on the pointer, because that would take what you typed with it. Clicking the quote must not make the card jump anywhere.
  6. Hover the quote nearest the top of the page. With no room above, the card flips below the words and the tail points up at them. Then check a quote near the left edge: the card overhangs the margin rather than being shoved sideways off the words it belongs to.
  7. Open a note from its dot instead. That one still opens in the right margin, because the dot is over there and that is where you were looking.
  8. Click a note's dot, then the </> on its card. The whole marker shows, closing --> and all, and the card keeps its buttons.
  9. Break a note on purpose — delete its -->, or type one into the middle. The edit is refused, the reason names the note, and what you typed is still in the box.
  10. Open the list that has a note inside it. Hovering above the note offers the items above it; below the note offers the item below. Neither leaves an empty bullet behind.
  11. Reach the control with Tab alone and open it with Enter. This is the one no automation reaches: a driven browser never has system focus, so :focus cannot match and Tab never arrives.
  12. Hover a block and press ⌫. The block goes, and no gap is left where it was. Then check the key does nothing while you are typing in an open editor, and nothing while text is selected — a selection means the popover is offering to comment on those words.
  13. Drag a block by the grip under the +. A line shows where it would land; dropping puts it there and nothing is glued to its new neighbour. Dragging a heading takes its whole section, and the numbers on ## 1. style headings are rewritten afterwards. Then check ⌥↑ / ⌥↓ does the same without a pointer.
  14. Drag the block that has a note on it. The note has to arrive with it — left behind, it does not orphan, it silently becomes a note about whatever is now above it.
  15. Try to start a drag from the middle of a paragraph. It must not start: selecting words is how a comment finds what it is about, and only the grip is draggable.
  16. Turn editing off (window.imark.setEditing(false) here, the Settings switch in the app). Both </> controls go, any open editor closes, and ⌫ stops deleting. Turn it back on and all three return.
